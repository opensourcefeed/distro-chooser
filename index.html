How the Distro Chooser works

Choosing a Linux distribution is mostly about fit: how much you want to tinker, what hardware you have and what you will use it for. The quiz turns those trade-offs into a short, ranked list.

  1. 1

    Answer 7 questions

    Tell us about your device, experience, hardware, graphics card, update preference and favourite desktop. It takes about a minute.

  2. 2

    We filter and score

    Distros that don't support your device are removed. Every other distro gets points from each answer, using weights we review by hand.

  3. 3

    See your matches and why

    You get your top picks with a match percentage, the answers that drove each one and any caveats, plus a link to a full guide.

How we score distributions

No sponsored placements and no black box. Here is exactly how a recommendation is made.

Hard filter for your device
If you pick a Raspberry Pi, an Apple Silicon Mac or a gaming handheld, only distros with official support for that hardware are ranked.
Hand-tuned weights
Each answer adds or removes points for each distro, on a scale from −5 to +5. "No preference" and "Not sure" answers never change the ranking.
An honest match percentage
Your match is a distro's score divided by the best score any distro could reach for your answers. 100% means it is the top pick on every answer.
Caveats you should know
Some distros come with important limits, such as a live-USB-only system or limited hardware support. We show these on the result card.
Reviewed data
Desktops, release models, NVIDIA support and project status are checked against each project's official sources. Last full review: 9 November 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Which Linux distro is best for beginners?

For most people new to Linux, Linux Mint, Ubuntu, Zorin OS and Pop!_OS are the safest starting points: they install easily, have large communities and handle drivers for you. The quiz weighs your hardware and preferences to pick between them.

Which Linux distro is best for gaming?

Pop!_OS and Ubuntu are solid general-purpose choices with good NVIDIA support. If gaming is your main use, newer gaming-focused distributions such as Bazzite, Nobara and CachyOS ship recent drivers, Steam and gaming tweaks out of the box. On a Steam Deck, SteamOS is the official choice.

Which Linux distro is best for an old laptop or PC?

Lightweight desktops make the biggest difference on old hardware. Lubuntu, Xubuntu, Linux Lite, MX Linux, antiX and Puppy Linux are built to run well with little memory. Pick "Old hardware revival" and your hardware age in the quiz to rank them.

Can I try a Linux distro without installing it?

Yes. Most distributions offer a live USB image: write it to a USB stick, boot from it and try the desktop without touching your current system. You can also test a distro in a virtual machine such as VirtualBox or GNOME Boxes.

Is the Distro Chooser free, and what happens to my answers?

The quiz is free and needs no account. Your answers are scored in your browser and are never sent to a server. They only appear in the page address so you can share or bookmark your results.

How often is the distro data updated?

Each distribution has a "last verified" date and the whole list is reviewed regularly against official project sources. The data was last reviewed on 9 November 2026. If you spot something out of date, please report it using the link in the footer.
