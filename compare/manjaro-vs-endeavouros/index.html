Manjaro vs EndeavourOS: which should you choose?

By the OpenSourceFeed team · Reviewed 9 November 2026

Short answer

Choose EndeavourOS if you want real Arch with an easy installer. Choose Manjaro if you prefer more graphical tools and updates held back in tested batches.

Manjaro and EndeavourOS are the two Arch-based distributions people weigh most often. Both offer a graphical installer, rolling updates and access to the Arch User Repository, and both offer KDE Plasma as a main edition.

Under the hood they take opposite approaches. EndeavourOS stays as close to Arch as possible; Manjaro builds its own layer on top. That shapes updates, compatibility and support.

Which one is right for you?

Choose Manjaro if…

  • You want graphical tools for kernels and drivers rather than terminal commands.
  • You like the idea of updates arriving in tested batches.
  • You want Pamac, a graphical package manager with optional Flatpak and AUR support.
  • You want editions for many desktops maintained by the project and its community.

Choose EndeavourOS if…

  • You want packages straight from Arch's repositories, on Arch's timing.
  • You use the AUR often and want it to work as on Arch.
  • You're comfortable with the terminal for updates and fixes.
  • You want the Arch Wiki to apply without exceptions.

Key differences

Where packages come from

EndeavourOS installs from Arch's repositories plus a small repository of its own tools. Manjaro uses its own repositories, moving Arch packages through unstable and testing branches before releasing them to stable, usually a couple of weeks after Arch.

AUR compatibility

AUR build scripts target current Arch, so they work as expected on EndeavourOS. On Manjaro, some AUR packages can fail until Manjaro's repositories catch up with Arch's versions.

Everyday tools

Manjaro is more graphical: Pamac for software, and a Settings Manager for kernels, drivers and language packs. EndeavourOS offers a Welcome app with update and maintenance shortcuts and the yay AUR helper, but expects more terminal use.

Philosophy

EndeavourOS calls itself terminal-centric and aims to be a lightly assisted Arch. Manjaro aims to be a user-friendly distribution in its own right, built on Arch technology. If you plan to learn Arch over time, EndeavourOS transfers more directly.

Desktop editions

Manjaro publishes official editions with KDE Plasma, GNOME and Xfce, plus community-maintained editions with other desktops, each with its own Manjaro theming. EndeavourOS has one installer that offers several desktops to choose from, with only light theming. Both let you add other desktops later.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for gaming?

Both run Steam and the latest drivers well. If gaming is your main use, a tuned Arch-based distro such as CachyOS may suit you better; see CachyOS vs EndeavourOS.

Which is easier for a newcomer?

Manjaro has more graphical tools, so it can feel easier at first. EndeavourOS has a welcoming community and simpler compatibility with Arch documentation. Total beginners are usually better off with a fixed-release distro.

Do both support NVIDIA?

Yes. Manjaro can install NVIDIA's driver from its Settings Manager; EndeavourOS can install it during setup or afterwards following the Arch Wiki.

Can I switch from Manjaro to EndeavourOS without reinstalling?

It isn't recommended. Manjaro's repositories and tools differ from Arch's, so converting a live system is error-prone. Back up your home folder, install EndeavourOS fresh and restore your files.

Which has better documentation?

EndeavourOS relies mainly on the Arch Wiki, which applies almost unchanged, plus its own wiki for its tools. Manjaro has its own wiki and forum; the Arch Wiki mostly applies too, except for kernels, drivers and anything affected by Manjaro's delayed packages.
